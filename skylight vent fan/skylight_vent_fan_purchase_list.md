## 5. Controls & automation

### Tier 1 — relay logic only (no smart home)

| Item | Why | Approx. |
|---|---|---|
| **DPDT relay, 120VAC coil, 10A contacts, + socket** | Reverses actuator polarity: energized = open, at rest = close | $15–30 |
| **Delay-on-make timer relay** | Delays the fan until the skylight has finished opening (essential under Path A, optional under Path B) | $20–40 |
| **Off-delay timer relay** | Only if your actuator has **no** internal end-of-travel cutoff — kills drive power ~60s after each move | $20–40 |
| **Junction boxes, DIN rail or enclosure** | Don't leave a PSU loose in a joist bay | $25–50 |

### Tier 2 — smart (recommended)

| Item | Why | Approx. | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shelly 2PM Gen3** (cover mode) | Purpose-built for reversing-motor loads: interlocked outputs, travel-time calibration, position tracking, direction reverse | $30–45 | [Shelly USA](https://us.shelly.com/products/shelly-2pm-gen3-1) · [Cover mode docs](https://kb.shelly.cloud/knowledge-base/cover-mode) |
| **Shelly Plus 1 / 1PM** | Controls and reports the fan so the automation knows its true state | $20–30 | [Shelly USA](https://us.shelly.com/) |
| **Humidity sensor** (Shelly H&T, Aqara, or an Aqara/Zigbee equivalent) | Enables the version you'll actually love: auto-run on humidity, not just the wall switch | $20–40 | Various |
| **Home Assistant host** (Green, Yellow, or a Raspberry Pi) | Only if you don't already run it. Shelly integrates natively | $0–130 | [HA Shelly integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/shelly/) |

> **Wiring caveat:** Shelly relay outputs are normally fed from the module's own line input. Switching a *separate* 24V supply needs the potential-free/dry-contact configuration — check your model's manual. If it isn't supported, either have the Shelly drive an external DPDT relay, or buy the **120V hardwired actuator** from §3 so the Shelly switches line voltage as designed.

## Ordering Sequence

1. **Nothing until §1 of the guide is done** — identify the unit, photograph the operator, measure the curb inside and out.
2. **Exercise the existing mechanism by hand.** If it binds, buy the operator/hinge parts from §8 and fix that first. An actuator will destroy itself against a seized hinge.
3. Order the **fan, duct, and roof cap** (§1) — longest lead on the physical work, and it's the part that has to be right.
4. Order the **actuator and rain sensor** (§3) once you've weighed the sash and know the force you need.
5. Order the **polycarbonate last**, after the grille location and duct drop are physically established.
6. Controls (§5) can be ordered any time — they're the easiest thing to change your mind about.
